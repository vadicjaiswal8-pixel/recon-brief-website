The Recon Brief Evidence Review Framework

A practical way to review research evidence before drawing conclusions.

Use five review questions to examine source quality, trace important claims to supporting evidence, distinguish independent evidence from repetition, surface uncertainty, and keep findings tied to the original research question.

!

This is a reader-led review framework, not a certification or a guarantee that every conclusion is correct.

CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
“A conclusion should never appear stronger than the evidence supporting it.”

Make the supporting material, uncertainty, assumptions, and limits of a conclusion visible before deciding how much weight to give it.

The evidence review framework

Five principles for
examining evidence.

Use these principles as complementary questions when reviewing a report. They support careful human judgement; they are not a scoring system.

01 / PRINCIPLE

Source Quality

Check whether a source fits the claim: who produced it, why, how, when, where, and on what evidence. Record its scope and limitations before giving it weight.

Questions to consider

  • Who published the information, and what is their basis for making the claim?
  • Is the underlying evidence or methodology available?
  • Is the source relevant to the geography, industry, and time period being examined?
  • Is this primary evidence or commentary based on another source?
Warning sign

A precise statistic is repeated without a traceable link to its original source.

02 / PRINCIPLE

Evidence Traceability

Trace important conclusions to identifiable supporting material. Separate what the source states from interpretation, and check that dates, definitions, numbers, and qualifications survive the summary.

Questions to consider

  • Can the original source be located?
  • Does the source actually support the claim attributed to it?
  • Have important numbers, dates, qualifications, or context been changed?
  • Can a reader independently inspect the supporting material?
Warning sign

A source discusses the general topic but does not substantiate the specific claim being made.

03 / PRINCIPLE

Independent Corroboration

Separate genuinely independent evidence from publications repeating one original claim. Compare publishers, methods, datasets, or first-hand observations where available.

Questions to consider

  • Do the sources rely on independent evidence?
  • Are multiple publications repeating a single original claim?
  • Where sources agree or disagree, what explains the difference?
  • Are alternative explanations worth examining?
Warning sign

Several articles repeating one original statistic are treated as several independent confirmations.

04 / PRINCIPLE

Uncertainty and Contradictions

Make material gaps, conflicting estimates, assumptions, and limitations visible. Differences in definitions, periods, or methods can change what a conclusion means.

Questions to consider

  • Do credible sources provide conflicting estimates?
  • Are they using different definitions, time periods, or methodologies?
  • What information is missing?
  • Which assumptions could materially change the conclusion?
Warning sign

A precise forecast is presented as certain despite conflicting evidence or significant assumptions.

05 / PRINCIPLE

Decision Relevance

Tie findings to the original question and keep recommendations within what the evidence supports. Broad context cannot substitute for company-, customer-, or market-specific evidence.

Questions to consider

  • Do the findings answer the original research question?
  • Are facts, assumptions, and interpretations clearly distinguished?
  • Does the recommendation follow from the evidence presented?
  • Is additional company-specific, customer-specific, or market-specific evidence needed?
Warning sign

Industry growth alone is used to claim that a particular company will succeed without examining evidence specific to that company.

Questions about the framework

Review the method
before the conclusion.

Short answers to common questions about using the Evidence Review Framework.

01Is the Evidence Review Framework a certification?⌄

No. It is a practical review framework, not an independent audit or certification, and it does not guarantee factual correctness.

02Does this framework verify every claim?⌄

No. It provides questions for reviewing source quality, supporting evidence, corroboration, uncertainty, and relevance; it does not by itself make a claim correct.

03How should I use the checklist?⌄

Start with the original research question, inspect important sources, trace claims to supporting material, distinguish independent evidence from repetition, note uncertainty and contradictions, and check that recommendations stay within what the evidence supports.
