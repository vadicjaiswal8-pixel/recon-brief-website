HOW IT WORKS

From a research question to a structured report.

Good research is more than collecting information. It involves finding relevant sources, understanding what they say, comparing findings, and presenting the results in a useful form.

The Recon Brief approach describes this process in five stages: Define, Research, Evidence, Analyze, and Report. The sequence is a conceptual way to keep the research objective visible as material is gathered, reviewed, interpreted, and organized.

The research workflow

Five stages from
question to understanding.

Each stage helps keep the objective visible while information is gathered, assessed, and organised.

01
DEFINE

Define

Frame the question worth answering.

Every useful investigation begins with a clear question. Define the topic, problem, decision, or research objective you want to understand.

A focused research question helps establish the scope of the investigation and makes it easier to judge whether the resulting report addresses the original objective.

Examples include:

  • What factors are influencing demand in a particular market?
  • How do competitors differentiate themselves?
  • What opportunities and risks exist in an industry?
  • What evidence supports or challenges a research hypothesis?
Key takeawayA well-defined question gives the research a clear direction.
02
RESEARCH

Research

Search across sources and materials.

Once the question is defined, identify and gather material that is relevant to it.

The available sources and inputs depend on the research task and the current application workflow; review their scope, date, authority, and limitations rather than treating collection as proof of completeness.

Consider the relevance, authority, recency, and completeness of the available sources. More information does not automatically mean better research.

Where possible, give careful attention to original documents, official publications, and primary data.

Key takeawayRelevant information provides the foundation for investigation.
03
EVIDENCE

Evidence

Examine supporting sources, gaps, and uncertainty.

Research findings become more useful when the material behind them can be located, compared, and examined.

Organise findings alongside relevant sources or supporting material so users can better understand where claims come from and what the available information supports.

Check whether a source actually supports the associated claim. Consider its credibility, publication date, relevance, and limitations.

When sources disagree or the evidence is incomplete, those differences should not be hidden. Source links alone do not guarantee independent verification. Important facts, figures, quotations, and recommendations should be checked against their original sources.

Key takeawayEvidence helps users evaluate the basis of a finding.
04
ANALYZE

Analyze

Compare findings, interpret patterns, and distinguish evidence from assumptions.

After gathering information and examining the supporting evidence, analyze what the findings may mean.

Look for patterns, differences, relationships, opportunities, risks, and unanswered questions. Compare relevant findings across sources and consider whether the available evidence supports the conclusions.

Distinguish observed information from estimates, assumptions, and projections. Where evidence is incomplete or contradictory, acknowledge the uncertainty rather than presenting an unsupported conclusion.

Analysis should help users move beyond a collection of facts towards a clearer understanding of the research question.

Key takeawayAnalysis connects individual findings to a broader understanding of the subject.
05
REPORT

Report

Organize findings, conclusions, limitations, and implications into a useful structure.

The final stage brings the research, supporting evidence, analysis, and remaining limits into an output that a reader can review and use.

Depending on the current application's supported functionality, this may include market research reports, competitive intelligence, company research, academic research material, research studies, or presentation preparation.

The goal is to make the findings easier to understand, review, and communicate.

A structured report can support decision-making, but it should not replace independent judgement. Review important claims, evidence, assumptions, and limitations before relying on the conclusions.

Key takeawayA structured report turns the investigation into an output people can use.
Choose your level of detail

Choose the depth that
matches your question.

Where a workflow offers report-depth choices, these three options describe different levels of detail for a focused question, a structured report, or a broader investigation.

01 / CONCISE

Quick Answer

A concise response for focused questions where a fast overview is sufficient.

02 / STRUCTURED

Standard Report

A structured report covering the core findings, supporting information, and analysis needed to understand the topic.

03 / COMPREHENSIVE

Deep Analysis

A more comprehensive investigation intended to provide broader evidence, comparisons, deeper reasoning, and discussion of limitations where available.

Quick Answer, Standard Report, and Deep Analysis describe intended scope—not a guarantee of stronger evidence. Current availability and details depend on the application and research task.

!
Responsible research

Use research as a starting point for informed decisions.

AI-assisted research can help organise information and surface useful findings, but it is not a substitute for independent judgement. Sources may be incomplete, information may change, and conclusions may require additional verification.

Review important evidence, check consequential claims against original sources, and acknowledge limitations before relying on a report.
