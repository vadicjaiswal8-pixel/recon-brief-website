The evidence layer

Sources are graded, not assumed.

A report that hides how it knows something is a guess with good formatting. These are the three habits that stop that.

01

Provenance stays close

Titles, domains, dates and source types sit next to the claim they support. In the sample, each source carries a label such as primary candidate or unverified.

E5Primary candidate
E12Primary candidate
E11Unverified
02

Claims keep their references

Cited evidence, estimates and interpretation are kept apart instead of blended into one answer, so you can see what each statement rests on.

Net loss of ₹28.15 croreE5, E12
$51 million Series DE11, E12
03

Gaps get their own section

The sample report ends with a section on research gaps and limitations, so what is missing is stated instead of smoothed over.
