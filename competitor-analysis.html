These examples are prompts for competitor research, not guaranteed report outputs. What can be explored depends on the research request and the evidence available for the company.

01 / OFFER

What products and services does this company offer?

Look for current, public descriptions of its products, services, packaging, and stated use cases.

02 / POSITION

How does it position itself in its market?

Examine the category, value, and alternatives the company emphasizes in its public messaging.

03 / CUSTOMERS

Which customer segments does it appear to serve?

Consider stated audiences, channels, and examples while remembering that public material may not show the full customer base.

04 / DEVELOPMENTS

What major business developments can be found in available sources?

Review dated announcements, filings, and reporting in context; an announcement alone does not establish its outcome.

05 / COMPARISON

What distinguishes its offering from other companies in the same category?

Compare specific, observable features against a defined set of alternatives rather than treating promotional claims as proven differences.

A research framework / five analytical lenses

The Recon Brief
Competitor Intelligence Framework

This framework organizes a company investigation from its identity to the decisions its evidence may inform. It is a research guide, not a claim that the application automatically performs every analysis or produces every kind of evidence below.

01 / ANALYTICAL LENS

Company Profile

Examines how the named business describes itself, the activities visible in public material, and the entities or locations that may need to be distinguished.

Questions to ask

  • How does the company describe its business and scope?
  • Do related brands, legal entities, subsidiaries, or regions need to be separated?

Useful evidence

Company and product pages, public filings or registry records where available, dated leadership statements, and credible reporting that identifies its sources.

Limitations

Company descriptions reflect the publisher's perspective and can become outdated. Private firms may disclose little, and similarly named entities can be confused.

02 / ANALYTICAL LENS

Offering Analysis

Examines the products and services a company publicly presents and how those offers are packaged, described, or priced when that information is available.

Questions to ask

  • What does the company explicitly offer now?
  • Which audience, need, or use case does each offer say it addresses?

Useful evidence

Current product and pricing pages, public documentation, release notes, and dated third-party coverage or reviews that can be checked in context.

Limitations

Offers can vary by region and date. Published descriptions do not establish adoption, quality, or customer outcomes, and reviews may not be representative.

03 / ANALYTICAL LENS

Positioning Analysis

Examines how the business frames its value, intended audience, category, and alternatives across its public communications.

Questions to ask

  • Which audiences and outcomes recur in its messaging?
  • Are claimed differentiators specific and testable, or mainly promotional language?

Useful evidence

Product pages, campaigns, public interviews, company statements, and independent coverage with a clear date and source.

Limitations

Messaging is evidence of how a company presents itself, not proof of customer perception or performance. A phrase can mean different things across markets.

04 / ANALYTICAL LENS

Market Context

Examines the defined category, geography, time period, and external conditions that shape how a company's position should be interpreted.

Questions to ask

  • What market boundaries and period are being compared?
  • Which external changes could affect demand, costs, regulation, or distribution?

Useful evidence

Official statistics, regulations, public industry research, and company or competitor disclosures whose dates and coverage can be compared.

Limitations

Market estimates often use different definitions. Broad trends can inform context but do not, by themselves, establish a result for one company.

05 / ANALYTICAL LENS

Strategic Implications

Examines what the reviewed findings might mean for a decision, while keeping observations separate from interpretation and assumptions.

Questions to ask

  • Which conclusions follow from evidence, and which depend on assumptions?
  • What missing information could change the next step?

Useful evidence

Cross-checked company and market sources, clearly scoped comparisons, and primary or customer research when it is actually available.

Limitations

Implications remain contextual and provisional. This lens is not a substitute for professional due diligence, financial advice, or comprehensive market research.

Questions about competitor research

Frequently Asked
Questions.

Use competitor research as a starting point for questions, not as an unquestioned verdict about a business.

01What is an AI competitor analysis tool?⌄

An AI competitor analysis tool helps users investigate businesses and organize information about competitors. The usefulness of its output depends on the research question, the available sources, and how carefully the findings are reviewed.

02Can I generate a report by entering a competitor's company name?⌄

A company name can be a useful starting point, but a meaningful investigation still needs a defined question, scope, relevant material, and review of supporting evidence. Whether the separate application can generate a particular output depends on its current workflow.

03What can a competitor research brief examine?⌄

A focused brief may examine company background, offerings, positioning, customer or market context, and questions for further research. Treat these as areas to investigate, not guaranteed findings, and review important claims against original sources.

04Is AI-generated competitor research always accurate?⌄

No. AI-generated research can contain gaps, outdated information, misinterpretations, or unsupported claims. Check consequential findings against their original sources and treat uncertainty as part of the result.

05How is competitor analysis different from market research?⌄

Competitor analysis focuses on particular businesses and their offerings or positioning. Market research examines a wider market, its customers, demand, and trends. The two can complement one another; see the AI Market Research page for a broader research perspective.

06Can competitor analysis support broader business research?⌄

Yes, competitor analysis can sit alongside market, company, or academic research when the questions overlap. Keep the scope distinct: a competitor lens examines particular businesses, while market research looks more broadly at customers, demand, and trends.
